\begin{abstract}
We examine throughput under varying load. A nonlinear response appears above a
critical rate, consistent with standard queueing bounds. The effect is stable
across three independent samples and is not an artifact of measurement
granularity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Prior work has focused on static loads, which cannot separate capacity
constraints from scheduling artifacts because the two produce observationally
equivalent steady states. We extend the analysis to dynamic regimes, where the
distinction becomes identifiable. Our contribution is threefold, and each part
depends on an apparatus whose calibration is unusually delicate, so we describe
it at length before turning to the estimation strategy that occupies the
remainder of this section.

\section{Model}
Let $\lambda$ denote the arrival rate and $\mu$ the service rate. The
utilization is
\begin{equation}
\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu}, \qquad 0 < \rho < 1 .
\end{equation}
Expected queue length follows the standard result
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[L] = \frac{\rho^{2}}{1-\rho} + \rho
  = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n (1-\rho)\rho^{n} .
\end{equation}
The bound is tight as $\rho \to 1^{-}$, which motivates the design below.

\section{Method}
Three conditions were run:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Baseline: nominal scheduling, no preemption.
  \item Treatment A: preemptive scheduling enabled.
  \item Treatment B: hybrid policy.
\end{itemize}
Each condition comprised roughly 120 trials, randomized within block.\footnote{We
thank two anonymous reviewers for helpful comments.}

\section{Discussion}
If the constraint were purely physical, preemption should not help; that it does
help implies a scheduling component.\footnote{Replication materials are archived
at the project site.} Future work should examine attenuation at higher loads.
